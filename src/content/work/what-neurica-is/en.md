Neurica is EONYX’s experimental space. Here we create and test prototypes, visual studies and working tools. We explore the path from an idea to something people can use: what it does, what data it relies on and which task it helps solve.

Neurica began as a psychological support project. We closed that direction and gave the space a broader role: exploring how technology, design and practical work come together.

Different stages of development belong here. A visual study can stand as a work in its own right. A prototype helps test a particular idea. A working pilot lets us try a tool on real tasks and see what needs improvement. Each work should clearly communicate its current state and limits of use.

ALTACO is our first such pilot. On Neurica, we are developing a material catalogue, selection through a shopping cart, and the generation of commercial offers and price lists. Behind these features is work on names, specifications, photographs, prices and calculation rules. Data quality matters as much as the usability of the interface.

Our approach is to test solutions in practice, record problems and preserve the decisions we make. Agreed features should do their job. If data needs confirmation or a capability has yet to be implemented, users should be able to see that. Being experimental does not remove responsibility for what a tool displays and calculates.

We plan to use the pilot’s tested mechanisms in ALTACO Platform. To prepare for that, we aim to separate data and business rules from the website’s presentation so they can evolve in another environment. Migration and scaling are future steps that the current work prepares us for.

This also lays the groundwork for future AI agents. They need organised data, clear rules and tested tools. We want agents to help people find information, prepare documents and carry out work tasks, with calculations handled by program logic and important decisions kept under human control.

Neurica opens this process to others: you can explore the work, try the available tools and take part in testing them. This helps us learn which ideas deserve further development and what they need to be useful in practice.
